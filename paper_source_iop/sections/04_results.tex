\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Site and wind}

The abbey stands \SI{\nRelief}{\meter} above the valley floor on a spur facing southwest
(aspect \ang{\nAspect}), sloping at a mean \ang{\nSlopeMean} and a 90th percentile of
\ang{\nSlopePninety} within \SI{\nSlopeRadius}{\meter} (\cref{fig:site}).
This southwest-facing ground runs \SI{\nLiftAlongSlope}{\meter} northwest from the abbey but
only \SI{\nLiftIntoWind}{\meter} southwest to the valley floor.

Over twenty years, \SI{\nFavPct}{\percent} of hours have southwest winds of
\SIrange{\nSpeedLo}{\nSpeedHi}{\meter\per\second} (\SI{95}{\percent} CI
\SIrange{\nFavLo}{\nFavHi}{\percent}), as do \SI{\nFavWinter}{\percent} of winter hours
(\cref{fig:wind}).
Southwest winds of \SI{\nSWStrong}{\meter\per\second} or more blow \nSWEightHours\ hours a year
(\SI{\nSWEightPct}{\percent}, CI \SIrange{\nSWEightLo}{\nSWEightHi}{\percent}).

In an \SI{\nRidgeWind}{\meter\per\second} wind the linear model (\cref{eq:ridge}) gives a
peak updraught of \SI{\nRidgePeak}{\meter\per\second} southwest of the abbey
(Supplementary \cref{fig:ridge}), close to the surface estimate
\(U\sin\theta_s = \SI{\nRidgeFormula}{\meter\per\second}\) at the 90th-percentile slope.
At its best glide the nominal craft sinks through still air at \(V/\sqrt{1+(\LD)^2}\):
\SI{\nSinkBest}{\meter\per\second} at \LD~\num{\nLDhi} and
\SI{\nSinkNominal}{\meter\per\second} at \LD~\num{\nLDnom}.
A strong wind can therefore hold up the better wings but not the poorer ones.

\subsection{Launch and glide}

Even a steady glide from the \SI{\nTower}{\meter} tower in still air would need
\LD~\num{\nLDStillSteady} to cover \SI{\nTarget}{\meter}, beyond the range of \cref{eq:polar}.
Eilmer starts from a jump, which is worse.
Diving from \SI{\nJump}{\meter\per\second} to \SI{\nTrimSpeed}{\meter\per\second} costs
\((V^2 - v_0^2)/2g \approx \SI{\nHeightToTrim}{\meter}\) of height, and the best wing
(\LD~\num{\nLDhi}) lands \SI{\nStillRangeBest}{\meter} out instead of the
\SI{\nStillRangeSteady}{\meter} of a steady glide (\cref{fig:glide}b).
A headwind supplies airspeed at release without the dive.

Flying into the wind, the same headwind slows him over the ground and the lift ends at the
foot of the slope.
No wing in \cref{fig:glide}a reaches \SI{\nTarget}{\meter} in winds up to
\SI{\nWindMax}{\meter\per\second}, and the longest flight is \SI{\nMaxRangeInto}{\meter} (dashed).
Along the slope a crabbing craft can stay in the lift.
There the best wing reaches \SI{\nTarget}{\meter} above \SI{\nMinWindBest}{\meter\per\second} and
the central wing above \SI{\nMinWindNominal}{\meter\per\second}; the poorest never does
(solid).
Lowering the tower from \SI{\nTowerHi}{\meter} to \SI{\nTowerLo}{\meter} raises the threshold for the best
wing from \SI{\nMinWindTowerHi}{\meter\per\second} to \SI{\nMinWindTowerLo}{\meter\per\second}.

\subsection{Pitch stability}

Without a tail the neutral point lies at \num{\nNPnotail}\,\(c\), ahead of every sampled CG,
so the wing is statically unstable in pitch (\cref{fig:stability}).
Pitch damping holds off divergence until the CG is aft of \num{\nCGonset}\,\(c\).
Across the sampled CG range a pitch disturbance doubles in \SI{\nTdoubleFwd}{\second} at
\num{\nCGlo}\,\(c\) and \SI{\nTdoubleAft}{\second} at \num{\nCGhi}\,\(c\)
(\SIrange{\nTdoubleInLo}{\nTdoubleInHi}{\second} for half or double the pitch inertia); a
\SI{\nTarget}{\meter} flight lasts about \SI{\nFlightTime}{\second}.
After an \ang{\nGust} gust the tailless craft passes \ang{\nPitchLoss} of pitch in \SI{\nTloss}{\second};
with a tail the gust dies away.

\subsection{Monte Carlo sample}

Into the wind none of the \nSamples\ cases reaches \SI{\nTarget}{\meter} (\SI{95}{\percent}
upper bound \SI{\nReachIntoHi}{\percent}; \cref{fig:montecarlo}a).
Along the slope \nReachSlopeN\ cases do, \SI{\nReachSlope}{\percent}
(CI \SIrange{\nReachSlopeLo}{\nReachSlopeHi}{\percent}), against a median range of
\SI{\nMedRangeSlope}{\meter}.
They share a strong wind and a good wing: a median launch wind of
\SI{\nMedWindReach}{\meter\per\second} (\SI{90}{\percent} above
\SI{\nTenWindReach}{\meter\per\second}) and a median glide ratio of \num{\nMedLDReach},
against \num{\nMedLDAll} over all cases.
In these flights the tailless doubling time has a median of
\SI{\nTdoubleMC}{\second} (99th percentile \SI{\nTdoubleMCpnn}{\second}), shorter than the
flight in every case (median \SI{\nMedFlightReach}{\second}; \cref{fig:montecarlo}b).
With a tail none diverges.

\subsection{Impact}

With a stopping distance \(d = \SI{\nStopMid}{\centi\meter}\) the legs break above about
\SI{\nOnsetMid}{\meter\per\second} of vertical speed, and across stopping distances and
fracture loads the onset runs from \SI{\nOnsetLo}{\meter\per\second} to
\SI{\nOnsetHi}{\meter\per\second} (\cref{fig:biomech}a).
A controlled glide that meets the ground without a flare arrives at a median
\SI{\nCtrlVzMed}{\meter\per\second} and at most \SI{\nCtrlVzMax}{\meter\per\second},
below the lowest onset, so a missed flare does not break the legs (\cref{fig:biomech}b).
After a loss of pitch control, at airspeeds of
\SIrange{\nVreachLo}{\nVreachHi}{\meter\per\second}, the median vertical speed is
\SI{\nDepVzMed}{\meter\per\second} and \SI{\nPbreakDep}{\percent} of arrivals exceed the
central onset.
The median deceleration over \SI{\nStopMid}{\centi\meter}, about \num{\nDecelG}\,\(g\), is commonly
survived in feet-first falls.
