\section{Conclusion}

A furlong's flight from the abbey tower is physically possible for a cloth wing, but only
in a strong southwest wind, with a wing near the better end of what the materials allow,
and on a track that follows the slope.
Without a tail the craft is unstable in pitch.
A disturbance doubles in about the time a man takes to react, so a flight of some tens of
seconds ending in a loss of control in a gust fits the models; a tail removes the
instability.
The broken legs need a fast, steep arrival, which a loss of control provides.
Eilmer's own explanation of his fall agrees with the physics, which cannot show that it was
the only cause.
